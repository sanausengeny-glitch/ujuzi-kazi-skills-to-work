# Ujuzi Kazi – Data Analysis Summary

## 1. Introduction
This report presents findings from the Ujuzi Kazi youth employment and skills-matching project.

The analysis uses two synthetic datasets:
- 10 youth profiles.
- 10 job advertisements.

## 2. Youth Employment Analysis

The youth employment distribution is:

| Employment Status | Number | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unemployed | 6 | 60% |
| Employed | 2 | 20% |
| Self-employed | 2 | 20% |

The sample indicates that unemployment is the largest category.

## 3. Skills Analysis

Youth profiles contain skills such as:
- Python
- Graphic Design
- Data Entry
- Digital Marketing
- Data Analytics
- Computer Skills
- Web Development
- Data Annotation

Employers request skills including Python, Excel, Canva, Data Annotation, Computer Skills and Content Creation.

Three exact skill matches were identified:
- Python
- Computer Skills
- Data Annotation

## 4. Employment Opportunities

The job advertisements include:
- Full-time employment
- Internships
- Freelance opportunities
- Contract opportunities

These represent different possible entry points into the labour market.

## 5. Geographic Analysis

The sample job advertisements cover:
- Nairobi
- Narok
- Kisumu
- Remote opportunities

Remote opportunities can provide additional access for young people outside major employment centres.

## 6. Recommendations

1. Promote practical technical skills training.
2. Connect young people with jobs matching their skills.
3. Increase access to internships and entry-level opportunities.
4. Encourage remote and freelance employment.
5. Strengthen collaboration between employers and training institutions.

## 7. Conclusion

Ujuzi Kazi demonstrates how data analysis can support youth employment by identifying skills, employer requirements and potential skills gaps.

The platform aims to make the connection between skills development and employment opportunities more accessible.

## 8. Data Limitations

The datasets contain only 10 synthetic records each. They are created for demonstration purposes and do not represent actual national employment statistics. Larger, verified datasets would be necessary for reliable real-world conclusions.
